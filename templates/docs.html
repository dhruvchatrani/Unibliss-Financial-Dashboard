KABA Brands Financial Dashboard

Your Amazon business, decoded. This dashboard turns raw Amazon settlement data into clear financial intelligence — so you know exactly where your money is going, what's profitable, and where to cut costs.

What This Dashboard Does

Every month, Amazon sends you a settlement — a lump sum bank transfer that covers multiple orders, fees, refunds, and adjustments. But Amazon doesn't give you a simple breakdown of "here's what you actually earned per product."

This dashboard does exactly that. You upload Amazon's CSV reports, and it automatically:

  • Categorises every transaction — orders, refunds, FBA fees, selling fees, advertising costs, reimbursements, and transfers
  • Calculates your true profit — not just revenue, but revenue minus every cost Amazon charges you
  • Breaks it down per SKU — so you know which products are actually making money and which are bleeding cash
  • Verifies Amazon's payout — catches discrepancies between what Amazon says they paid you and what they actually owe

In short: this dashboard answers the question every Amazon seller asks — "After everything Amazon takes, how much money am I actually making?"

How to Upload Your Data

Before You Start

You need to download reports from Amazon Seller Central. Go to Reports → Payments → Statement View for settlement data, and Reports → Business Reports for session/conversion data.

Step 1: Go to Upload

Navigate to the Upload page. Select the month these reports belong to (this is used for the Business Report and Ads Report imports).

Step 2: Upload the Settlement Report (Required)

This is the most important file. It contains every financial transaction Amazon processed for your account.

Where to find it: Seller Central → Payments → Statement View → Download → Choose "Detailed" format

File name: Usually contains "Monthly Report" or "Transaction_All Transactions.csv"

What it contains: Every order, refund, fee, reimbursement, and transfer — with dates, amounts, and SKU details

Multi-month support: You can upload a CSV that covers multiple months. The system auto-detects the date range from the data — no need to upload one month at a time.

Step 3: Upload Optional Reports

Optional

Deferred Transactions

Orders Amazon is holding back because the return window hasn't closed yet. This helps the Reconciliation page figure out if Amazon's payout is correct. Without this file, reconciliation may show false discrepancies.

Optional

Business Report

Shows sessions (traffic), page views, and unit session percentage (conversion rate) per SKU. Found under Reports → Business Reports → Detail Page Sales and Traffic by Child Item. This powers the "Account CVR" and per-SKU session data on the dashboard.

Optional

Advertising Report

Sponsored Products search term report. Shows which keywords are driving sales, how much you're spending per click, and your advertising cost of sales (ACOS). Found under Advertising → Sponsored Products → Reports.

Step 4: Click "Upload & Sync to Dashboard"

Everything gets processed in one go. You'll be redirected to the Dashboard with all your data updated.

Dashboard — Your Business at a Glance

The Dashboard is your home screen. It shows the big picture of your Amazon business for any date range.

Key Metrics (Top Row)

Total Sales

Gross product sales from orders (excluding refunds and deferred). This is the top-line revenue Amazon reported for your products.

Net Income

What you actually earned after ALL costs — FBA fees, selling fees, refunds, advertising, COGS, operating expenses, and promotions. This is the real bottom line.

COGS (Cost of Goods Sold)

What your inventory costs you — manufacturing, sourcing, or purchase price per unit × units sold. Set this up in the Admin page.

Ads Cost

Total Sponsored Products spend. Pulled from "Cost of Advertising" entries in your settlement report.

Refunds

Total amount refunded to customers. High refunds eat into margins — keep an eye on this.

Units Sold

Total units ordered (not including refunds). Helps calculate per-unit profitability.

True Profit / Sale

Net Income ÷ Units Sold. How much you actually earn per unit after every cost. If this is negative, you're losing money on every sale.

Account CVR (Conversion Rate)

Percentage of visitors who actually buy. Calculated from Business Report data. Industry benchmark: 10-15% for most categories.

Promos & Deals

Total promotional rebates (coupons, deals, Lightning Deals) automatically deducted from settlement data. This is included in Net Income calculations.

Derived Metrics (Second Row)

Gross Margin %

(Sales + Reimbursements − Refunds − COGS − All Fees − Promotions) ÷ Sales. Shows how much of your revenue stays with you after direct costs. Below 15% is a red flag.

Net Margin %

Net Income ÷ Sales. Your true profit margin after EVERYTHING — including ads and operating expenses. This is what matters.

TACOS (Total Advertising Cost of Sales)

Total Ad Spend ÷ Total Sales. Measures how much of your total revenue goes to advertising. Below 10% is healthy; above 15% means ads are eating your profits.

ACOS (Advertising Cost of Sales)

Ad Spend ÷ Ad-Attributed Sales. How efficient your ads are at generating sales. Below 20% is great; above 35% needs optimisation.

Return Rate %

Total Returns ÷ Units Sold. High return rates kill profitability. Target: below 3%. If above 5%, investigate product quality or listing accuracy.

ROI (Return on Investment)

Net Income ÷ Total COGS. How much return you get on every rupee invested in inventory. Above 20% is healthy.

Period Comparisons

The comparison table shows how your current period stacks up against the previous month (MoM) and the same period last year (YoY). Use this to spot trends — are your margins improving? Is your ad spend creeping up? Are seasonal patterns visible?

Charts

Daily Sales Trend — shows your daily revenue over time. Spikes indicate high-selling days; dips may signal stock issues or listing problems.

Disbursements — shows Amazon's bank transfers to you. These should roughly match your expected payout (see Reconciliation page).

SKU Insights — Product-Level Profitability

While the Dashboard shows account-wide numbers, SKU Insights breaks it down per product. This is where you find out which SKUs are actually profitable and which are dead weight.

How SKU Profitability Works

Each product (SKU) has its own revenue and costs. Amazon tracks orders, refunds, FBA fees, and selling fees at the SKU level. The system calculates:

SKU Net Profit = SKU Sales − SKU FBA Fees − SKU Selling Fees − SKU Refunds − SKU Other Fees − SKU COGS

SKU Margin % = SKU Net Profit ÷ SKU Sales × 100

SKU ROI % = SKU Net Profit ÷ SKU Total COGS × 100

SKU Profit / Sale = SKU Net Profit ÷ Units Ordered

OpEx Allocation

Operating expenses (rent, software, salaries, etc.) are allocated to each SKU proportionally based on revenue contribution. If a SKU generates 40% of your revenue, it bears 40% of your overhead. This gives you the "True Net Profit" — what each product earns after its fair share of business costs.

Trend Columns (WoW / MoM / YoY)

Each SKU shows week-over-week, month-over-month, and year-over-year sales changes. Use these to spot:

  • Declining products — WoW and MoM both negative = investigate (stock issues? listing suppressed? competitor undercutting?)
  • Seasonal products — YoY spike = seasonal demand, plan inventory accordingly
  • Rising stars — consistent positive trends = consider increasing ad spend

Cashflow Health

A SKU is marked "Low" if its Blended Profit per Unit is less than its COGS. This means the revenue from that product doesn't even cover the cost of making/buying it — you're literally paying to sell it. Consider raising the price, cutting ad spend on it, or discontinuing it.

Advertising — Ad Performance

The Advertising page shows how your Sponsored Products campaigns are performing, pulled from Amazon's search term report.

Key Ad Metrics

Impressions

How many times your ad was shown. High impressions with low clicks = weak creative or wrong targeting.

Clicks

How many times someone clicked your ad. Clicks ÷ Impressions = CTR (Click-Through Rate).

Spend

Total amount spent on ads. This is your advertising cost for the period.

Ad Sales

Revenue generated directly from ad clicks. Spend ÷ Ad Sales = ACOS.

CTR (Click-Through Rate)

Clicks ÷ Impressions. Shows how compelling your ad is. Below 0.3% needs better images/title.

CVR (Conversion Rate)

Orders ÷ Clicks. How many clicks turn into sales. Below 8% means your listing needs work (images, bullets, reviews).

Search Terms

The search terms table shows exactly what customers typed into Amazon before clicking your ad. This is gold for keyword optimisation — double down on high-converting terms, negate irrelevant ones.

Reconciliation — Did Amazon Pay You Correctly?

Amazon's settlement system is complex. They deduct fees, hold reserves, delay some payments, and sometimes make errors. The Reconciliation page verifies that the money hitting your bank account matches what you're entitled to.

How It Calculates "What Amazon Should Have Paid You"

Net Order Proceeds = Total Order Amount (all orders including deferred)

− Refunds = Refunds issued to customers

+ Reimbursements = Money Amazon paid you back (damaged inventory, overcharges, etc.)

− Other Fees = Shipping services, miscellaneous charges

− Account Reserve = General holdback Amazon maintains (enter this manually)

− Deferred Transactions = Orders still in the return window (not yet paid out)

= Expected Disbursement

Then: Discrepancy = Expected Disbursement − Actual Disbursement (what Amazon actually transferred to your bank).

Understanding the Result

OK

Discrepancy is under ₹100 (configurable threshold). This is normal rounding — Amazon's payout matches.

CHECK

Discrepancy is above threshold. Amazon either owes you money or overpaid. Investigate using the Deep Dive table below.

Deferred Transactions Explained

When a customer buys a product, Amazon doesn't immediately pay you. They hold the money during the return window (typically 30 days) to cover potential refunds. These held amounts are "deferred."

  • The dashboard excludes deferred from your revenue — you haven't received the money yet, so it's not counted as income
  • The reconciliation subtracts deferred from expected payout — Amazon is right to not have paid you this yet
  • When Amazon eventually pays out those deferred orders, the money shows up as a Transfer — but the system may show a temporary positive discrepancy since it doesn't track which specific deferred orders were paid

Account Reserve

Amazon holds a percentage of your account as a general reserve against chargebacks and high-value returns. This is different from order-level deferment — it's a blanket hold. Check your Reserve Details in Seller Central to find this number, then enter it in the Reconciliation page filter.

Deep Dive Table

The detailed payout history breaks down each bank transfer by its component parts — gross sales, fees, promotions, refunds, and reimbursements — so you can trace exactly where the money went for each payout cycle.

How Net Income Is Calculated

Every number on this dashboard flows from one master formula. Here's the exact breakdown:

+ Sales (product sales from orders)

+ Reimbursements (Amazon paying you back)

− FBA Fees (warehousing, picking, packing, shipping)

− Selling Fees (Amazon's referral/commission fee)

− Other Amazon Fees (storage, labels, processing)

− Refunds (customer returns)

− Advertising Cost (Sponsored Products spend)

− COGS (cost of making/buying the product)

− Operating Expenses (rent, software, salaries, etc.)

− Promotions (coupons, deals, rebates)

= Net Income (your true profit)

Note: Deferred transactions are excluded from all calculations until they're actually disbursed.

Understanding Amazon's Fee Types

Amazon charges multiple fees on every sale. Here's what each one means:

FBA Fees (Fulfillment by Amazon)

Charged when you use FBA. Covers picking, packing, shipping, and customer service. Varies by product size and weight. Typically 20-35% of sale price.

Selling Fees (Referral Fee)

Amazon's commission — usually 15% of sale price (varies by category). This is what you pay for access to Amazon's marketplace.

Other Amazon Fees

Includes monthly storage fees, label fees, long-term storage penalties, removal orders, and miscellaneous charges. These add up — check monthly.

Promotional Rebates

Discounts from coupons, Lightning Deals, and promotions. These reduce your revenue directly. Tracked separately so you can see the true cost of each promotion.

Admin — Setup & Maintenance

COGS (Cost of Goods Sold)

Set the per-unit cost for each SKU. This is critical — without accurate COGS, your profit numbers are meaningless. Update whenever your supplier pricing changes.

Local Inventory

Track your local stock levels separate from Amazon's FBA inventory. Helps you plan replenishments and avoid stockouts.

Operating Expenses

Log business overhead — software subscriptions, rent, salaries, packaging, etc. These get allocated proportionally to each SKU for true profitability analysis.

Backup Database

Download a backup of all your data. Do this regularly — especially before uploading large amounts of data.
